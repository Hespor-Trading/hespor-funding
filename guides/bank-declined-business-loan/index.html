Declined for a business loan by your bank? Your next steps

By HESPOR Funding · Updated November 2026

Why banks say noWhat to do nextHow alternative funding differsThe honest trade-offGet your options

Short answer: Banks often decline small businesses for short credit history, lack of collateral or strict ratios. Alternative lenders look mainly at revenue and time in business, so a declined business with steady sales can often still get funding, at a higher cost than a bank.

Why banks say no

  • Limited business credit history.
  • Not enough collateral or personal assets.
  • Debt ratios or profitability below the bank's thresholds.
  • An industry the bank considers higher risk, such as restaurants.
  • Not enough time in business for the bank's policy.

What to do next

  1. Ask the bank exactly why, and whether a smaller amount or a government-backed program would work.
  2. Decide how urgent the need is. If waiting costs you a season or a contract, speed has real value.
  3. Compare the total cost of faster options, not just the speed.

How alternative funding differs

Alternative lenders focus mainly on your current revenue and time in operation. With our lending partner, businesses in Canada with 6+ months in operation and $10,000+ monthly revenue can get a decision in as little as one business day.

The honest trade-off

Fast funding costs more than a bank loan. It makes sense when the opportunity or problem in front of you is worth more than the extra cost, for example inventory that sells at a margin, or equipment that keeps you open.
