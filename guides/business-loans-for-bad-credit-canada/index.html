Business loans for bad credit in Canada: what lenders actually look at

By HESPOR Funding · Updated November 2026

Does bad credit rule you out?

Not automatically. Banks lean heavily on credit history and collateral. Alternative lenders such as our lending partner focus mainly on how your business is performing today: how much revenue comes in each month and how long you have been operating. A weak personal score is one part of the picture, not the whole of it.

It is still a review, not a promise. Every decision belongs to the lender.

What the lender looks at

  • Monthly revenue: your average monthly revenue, usually reviewed through a secure, read-only connection to your business bank account.
  • Time in business: at least 6 months in operation.
  • Location: a business located in Canada.
  • The whole picture: your overall business performance, plus other factors the lender may consider, such as existing loans or advances.

The minimum requirements

To request funding options, your business must be located in Canada, operating for at least 6 months and earning at least $10,000 a month. Meeting the minimums does not guarantee approval.

Will you check my credit?

Checking your options starts with a soft credit inquiry by our lending partner, which does not affect your credit score. A hard credit check happens only if you decide to move forward with funding.

What it costs

Fast funding costs more than a bank loan. Business loans and merchant cash advances through our lending partner are priced with a factor rate (typically 1.10 to 1.50), not an interest rate. You repay the amount funded multiplied by the factor, and the total is fixed upfront: for example, $50,000 × 1.20 = $60,000, whether it is repaid over 6, 12 or 24 months.

Your exact factor rate comes from the lender after its review and is shown in your offer before you accept. Try the cost calculator and compare with your other options before you decide.

Ways to strengthen your request

  • Run your sales through your business bank account so the lender can see your real revenue.
  • Have your business details and ID ready so the process moves quickly.
  • Be upfront about any existing loans or advances.
  • Ask for an amount that fits your revenue rather than the maximum.

None of this guarantees approval, but clear and accurate information helps the lender review your business.

If you are not there yet

If your business is under 6 months old or below $10,000 a month, government-backed programs or your bank may be better routes for now. You can still send your details and we will tell you honestly whether there is an option. See also what to do when a bank declines you.
