Merchant cash advance vs business loan: which is right for you?

By HESPOR Funding · Updated November 2026

How each one worksSide-by-side comparisonWhen a business loan is the better choiceWhen a merchant cash advance is the better choiceWhat both costNot sure? Let the lender recommend

Short answer: A business loan has fixed payments and suits planned investments. A merchant cash advance can be repaid as a share of card sales and suits businesses with steady card revenue and uneven months. Both can be priced with a factor rate, so the total cost is fixed upfront.

How each one works

A business loan (term financing) gives you a lump sum that you repay through automatic daily or weekly payments over a set term, typically 6 to 24 months. A merchant cash advance gives you a lump sum in exchange for a share of your future card sales, repaid until an agreed total is reached.

Side-by-side comparison

Business loanMerchant cash advance
RepaymentFixed daily or weekly paymentsA share of card sales, or fixed daily/weekly

When a business loan is the better choice

  • You know exactly what you are buying, such as equipment or a renovation.
  • You want predictable payments you can budget around.
  • Your revenue is not mainly card-based, such as contractors or wholesalers.

When a merchant cash advance is the better choice

  • Most of your revenue comes through card payments.
  • Your sales swing with the seasons and you prefer payments that follow them.
  • You want approval based mainly on sales history.

What both cost

Both are commonly priced with a factor rate. Multiply the amount funded by the factor to get the total repayment: $50,000 at 1.20 means $60,000. Factor rates typically range from 1.10 to 1.50. Both cost more than a bank loan; they exist for speed and accessibility.
